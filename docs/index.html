Cheap, harm-related actions become the ritual

One time step

1 · Eq. 2–3

Threat belief

Triggers push the harm signal h up; it relaxes back toward 0. p(harm) is its scaled logistic.

2 · Eq. 5

State

Anxious when p(harm) exceeds the threshold, calm otherwise. Each state has its own policy.

3 · Eq. 4

Expectation

The critic’s value is the expected harm: V = p(harm) · v(harm).

4 · Eq. 6–7

Action

One of ten actions, by softmax over propensities θ minus accumulated repetition cost.

5 · Eq. 1

Outcome

A catastrophe with probability freq(c), otherwise nothing. The action has no influence.

6 · Eq. 8–9

Prediction error

A = r − V. When harm was expected and nothing happened, A > 0 and the chosen action is strengthened.

7 · Eq. 10

Relief

The more confident the choice, the faster the threat belief relaxes.

Implementation notes

  • Ten actions; propensities θ and costs start at 0; calm and anxious states keep separate policies.
  • Triggers arrive on the first step and then on every multiple of the trigger interval (steps 100, 200, …), as in the simulation code.
  • A catastrophe changes the reward only. It does not feed back into the threat belief.
  • Repetition cost is charged to the chosen action in the current state; all costs decay by fp each step.
  • Ritual frequency is the share of a state’s steps taken by that state’s most-chosen action.
  • In Simulation 3 all agents in a sample share one set of ten action values, read from Figure 3. As in the simulation code, each agent draws its own v(harm) ~ N(−100,5) and freq(c) ~ Beta(1,1000). Ritual frequency there is each action’s share of anxious-state choices, averaged over agents as in the analysis code.
